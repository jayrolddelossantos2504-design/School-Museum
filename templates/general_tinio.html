Born in Licab, Nueva Ecija on June 17, 1877, Manuel Tinio came from an illustrious Novo Ecijano lineage. At the youthful age of nineteen, when the flames of the 1896 Philippine Revolution ignited across Central Luzon, Tinio gathered local patriots, demonstrating tactical brilliance and unyielding defense of national sovereignty.

Recognizing his courage at the Battle of San Miguel and across the rugged cordilleras, General Emilio Aguinaldo elevated Manuel Tinio to Brigadier General at just twenty years old. He was entrusted with the crucial command of all revolutionary forces in Northern Luzon—becoming affectionately known as the "Hero of the North."

This school carries his name as a charge: that scholarship and character belong together, and that education is itself a form of public service. The heritage committee curates his memorabilia, portraits, and correspondence as the anchor of the museum's permanent collection.
